## CPF refund when selling or transferring property

**Notice**

Upon the sale or transfer of your property, the sale proceeds will be used to pay off the outstanding housing loan taken and refund the CPF amount you used for your property.

You can check the amount to be refunded on the "What Happens If" section on your Home ownership dashboard if you were to sell your property now.

The amount that you have to refund is the CPF principal amount you withdrew (P) and the accrued interest (I). If you are aged 55 or above and have pledged your property to set aside your retirement sum, you will also need to refund the pledged amount.

**IMPORTANT NOTE:**

However, please note that your required CPF refund is less than your P+I if you:

1. were aged 55 or older before 1 January 2013; and
2. have set aside your Full Retirement Sum (FRS) before 1 January 2013.

This is because having set aside your FRS before 1 January 2013, you are not required by law to refund the CPF you have used to pay for your property prior to 2013. If you have continued to use your CPF from 1 January 2013, only the principal amount used from 1 January 2013, and the accrued interest need to be refunded when you sell your property.

If you are below 55, your housing refunds will be credited to your Ordinary Account (OA). If you are above 55, your housing refunds will be first used to top up your Retirement Account (RA) to meet your required retirement sum and the balance refunds will remain in your OA. You can use your OA savings to:

- Pay for another property or participate in other CPF schemes.
- Keep the savings in your CPF accounts to earn risk-free interest rate. Find out more about CPF interest rates.
- Get higher payouts in retirement by transferring the OA balance to your SA (if you are below 55) or RA (if you are age 55 and above).
- Withdraw your CPF savings for immediate retirement needs if you are above age 55.

If you are aged 55 and above, and plan to buy a 3-room or smaller HDB flat, you can use your RA savings in excess of your Basic Retirement Sum for your next property purchase. You can qualify if:

1. You purchase the flat within 3 years after selling this property
2. The new flat costs less than what you sold this property for
3. You have not been issued with a CPF LIFE plan, so there are available savings in your RA

Check the amount available for use on your Home ownership dashboard. Please ensure that the funds are available in your RA when you need them for the purchase.

If you have sold the property at market value but the selling price of your property is not enough to cover the outstanding housing loan and the required CPF housing refund, you will only need to refund the selling price less the outstanding housing loan to your CPF account. You do not need to top up the CPF housing refund shortfall in cash, as long as the property is sold at market value.

Any option money (such as an option fee or option exercise fee) you receive from the buyer in cash is considered part of the selling price and must be refunded to your CPF account before the transaction can be completed.

If you are selling your share of a property to someone else, the refund amount may be different.

Part share selling price – x% of outstanding housing loan

(where x% = your share of property sold)

Your required CPF refund/total required CPF refund of you and your co-owner x balance sale proceeds

(where balance sale proceeds = selling price of entire property minus the outstanding housing loan)

To reduce the amount you need to refund when you sell your property, consider **making voluntary housing refunds**. Voluntary housing refunds allows you to enjoy the attractive interest rates of your CPF savings.
