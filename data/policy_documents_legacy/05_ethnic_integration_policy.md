## 1. Background & Legal Mandate of the Ethnic Integration Policy (EIP)

Enacted on **1 March 1989** under the Housing and Development Act, the Ethnic Integration Policy (EIP) is one of Singapore's central social cohesion policies. Its statutory objective is to prevent the racial segregation and formation of mono-ethnic enclaves across public housing estates, ensuring that HDB towns reflect Singapore's multi-racial demographic balance.

### 1.1 Quota Structure & Thresholds
EIP limits are enforced at two distinct spatial geographic tiers:
1. **Neighbourhood Level:** Enforces macro-level demographic balance across the entire electoral/precinct planning area.
2. **Block Level:** Enforces hyper-local demographic balance within each individual residential HDB building.

While exact percentages are calibrated periodically to match national census trends, the statutory ceiling thresholds are approximately:
| Ethnic Group | Maximum Neighbourhood Limit | Maximum Individual Block Limit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chinese** | ~**84%** of total units | ~**87%** of total units |
| **Malay** | ~**22%** of total units | ~**25%** of total units |
| **Indian & Other Races** | ~**12%** of total units | ~**15%** of total units |

### 1.2 Monthly Quota Updates
- EIP eligibility figures are dynamic and updated on the **1st of every month** on HDB InfoWEB.
- An Option to Purchase (OTP) is legally granted and accepted based on the prevailing EIP status on the date the OTP is granted.

## 2. Resale Transaction Rules Under EIP

When a flat owner attempts to sell their HDB flat on the open resale market, the transaction must comply with the prevailing EIP status of the block and neighbourhood.

### 2.1 The Permissibility Test
1. **Transaction Allowed:** If the buyer's ethnic group is **within quota** for both the block and the neighbourhood, the sale proceeds unimpeded.
2. **Transaction Blocked:** If the quota for the prospective buyer's ethnic group has been reached (i.e., is full) in either the block or the neighbourhood, the transaction is **strictly prohibited by law**.

### 2.2 The Same-Ethnicity Transaction Exception
A critical, foundational statutory rule governs same-race sales:
- **The Rule:** If the seller and the prospective buyer belong to the **same ethnic group** (e.g., Chinese seller selling to a Chinese buyer, Malay seller to a Malay buyer, or Indian seller to an Indian buyer), the transaction is **ALWAYS PERMITTED**, even if the quota for that ethnic group is completely full at both the block and neighbourhood levels.
- **Statutory Rationale:** A sale between members of the same ethnic group results in zero net change to the racial proportions of the block or neighbourhood, and therefore does not violate the legislative intent of the EIP.

### 2.3 Mixed-Race Households & Inter-Ethnic Marriages
To accommodate Singapore's growing number of multi-ethnic households:
- **Election of Race:** A mixed-race married couple (or an applicant who is double-barrelled, e.g. Chinese-Indian) can choose to be classified under **either spouse's official ethnic group** for the purpose of the EIP check.
- **Tenure Commitment:** Once an ethnic classification is elected and approved at the time of flat purchase, that declared ethnic classification binds the household for the entire duration of flat ownership and applies upon future resale.

## 3. Singapore Permanent Resident (SPR) Quota

In addition to racial quotas under the EIP, public housing estates are subject to an independent **Singapore Permanent Resident (SPR) Quota** introduced in 2010.

### 3.1 Targeted Scope
- The SPR quota applies strictly to households where **all buyers and occupiers are Singapore Permanent Residents** (i.e., non-citizen households without any Singapore Citizen member).
- Households comprising at least one Singapore Citizen are **exempt** from the SPR quota.

### 3.2 Statutory Ceilings
| Geographic Tier | Maximum Non-Malaysian SPR Proportion |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Neighbourhood Level** | **5%** of total units |
| **Block Level** | **8%** of total units |

### 3.3 Exemption for Malaysian Permanent Residents
- Due to deep historical, geographic, linguistic, and cultural commonalities, **Malaysian Permanent Residents are exempt from the SPR quota**.
- A household consisting purely of Malaysian SPRs can purchase a flat provided they satisfy standard EIP racial quotas.

## 4. The EIP Buyback Assistance Scheme

Because EIP quotas can restrict the pool of eligible buyers for flat owners belonging to minority ethnic groups or owners in popular precincts where minority quotas are filled, owners occasionally face liquidity constraints or price discounts.

To address this structural market friction, MND and HDB established the **EIP Buyback Assistance Scheme** on **8 March 2022**.

### 4.1 Statutory Eligibility Criteria
Flat owners who face genuine difficulties selling their flat due to EIP constraints can apply to have **HDB purchase the flat back directly**. To qualify, the owner must satisfy all four statutory criteria:
1. **Fulfillment of MOP:** The owner must have completed the required Minimum Occupation Period (5 years for Standard, 10 years for Plus/Prime).
2. **Continuous Active Marketing for At Least 6 Months:** The flat must have been actively listed and marketed on major property portals (e.g. PropertyGuru, 99.co, EdgeProp) for a minimum of **6 continuous months**.
3. **Realistic Valuation Benchmark:** The asking price throughout the 6-month marketing period must not have exceeded the prevailing market valuation for comparable flats in the same neighbourhood (as verified by recent HDB transaction data).
4. **Demonstrated Quota Constraint:** The seller must provide evidence that they received interest or offers from potential buyers who were legally blocked solely because the buyers' ethnic group quota was full.

### 4.2 Buyback Terms & Valuation
- **Fair Market Price:** When an application is approved, HDB appoints independent licensed professional valuers to appraise the flat. HDB buys the flat at **prevailing fair market valuation**.
- **Financial Flexibility Options:**
  - **Deferred Downpayment Scheme:** Sellers purchasing a new or resale flat concurrently can defer downpayment until keys are collected.
  - **Temporary Extension of Stay:** Sellers can request a temporary extension of stay for up to **3 months** after completion to facilitate smooth renovation and moving into their subsequent home.
